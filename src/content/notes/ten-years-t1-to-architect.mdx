In June 2016 I put on a headset and started answering support calls for a web hosting company. Ten years later I architect the infrastructure behind hundreds of banking websites. There was no degree in between. There were books, online courses, and a lot of production systems.

The ranks on this site are a nod to Solo Leveling, but the climb was real. Here's what each stage taught me.

## E-rank: T1 tech support

Front-line support for shared, VPS, and dedicated hosting. Websites, email, databases, DNS, all day, every day. Tracing mail delivery through Splunk logs taught me the first real lesson: **the logs usually know what happened.** Customers describe symptoms; the system records causes.

## E+: Web Concierge

I taught customers to build their own sites on WordPress, Joomla, and Drupal. Explaining technical things to people who don't care about the technology turned out to be a skill I'd use for the rest of my career, right up to explaining trade-offs to a CEO.

## D-rank: Professional Services

Paid engineering work: site and mailbox migrations, custom PHP forms, third-party installs. Migrations teach you to plan for the thing you forgot, because there is always a thing you forgot.

## C-rank: T3 support

The escalation point. When upper management or the legal team had a problem, it landed here. You learn to stay calm, gather facts, and close the loop in writing.

## B-rank: Systems administrator

A fleet of cPanel and CentOS servers, plus self-managed hosts on KVM-OpenStack and OpenVZ. This is where I started writing Bash tools to avoid doing the same thing twice.

## A-rank: Linux engineer

Ansible and Jenkins at a life-sciences company, plus independent Linux administration contracts. Automation stopped being a convenience and became the way I build everything.

## S-rank: Sr. Systems Architect

Today I lead infrastructure across a primary datacenter and a disaster-recovery hot site, with a 99.99% uptime target, PCI-DSS compliance, and the tooling to run it in Go and Python.

## What carried over

Every stage built on the last one. Support taught me to read systems. Teaching taught me to explain them. Administration taught me to automate them. Architecture is all three at once.

If you're on the phones right now wondering whether you can get from here to there without a degree: you can. Read the logs, write things down, and automate the second time.
